## Textbook chapter map

### Chapter 1: Probability Theory

1.1 Set Theory; 1.2 Basics of Probability Theory; 1.3 Conditional Probability and Independence; 1.4 Random Variables; 1.5 Distribution Functions; 1.6 Density and Mass Functions.

### Chapter 2: Transformations and Expectations

2.1 Distributions of Functions of a Random Variable; 2.2 Expected Values; 2.3 Moments and Moment Generating Functions; 2.4 Differentiating Under an Integral Sign.

### Chapter 3: Common Families of Distributions

3.1 Introduction; 3.2 Discrete Distributions; 3.3 Continuous Distributions; 3.4 Exponential Families; 3.5 Location and Scale Families; 3.6 Inequalities and Identities.

### Chapter 4: Multiple Random Variables

4.1 Joint and Marginal Distributions; 4.2 Conditional Distributions and Independence; 4.3 Bivariate Transformations; 4.4 Hierarchical Models and Mixture Distributions; 4.5 Covariance and Correlation; 4.6 Multivariate Distributions; 4.7 Inequalities.

## Planning notes

- Adding Chapter 4 compresses the schedule: Chapter 1 now ends in Week 3, and Chapter 2 in Week 5. The midterm covers Chapters 1–2.
- Chapter 3 gets about three lectures (Weeks 7–8). Students have seen most of the named families in STAT 160, so lectures emphasize derivations, relationships among families, exponential and location–scale structure, rather than cataloguing pmfs and pdfs.
- Chapter 4 gets about four lectures (Weeks 8–10). §2.4 (differentiating under an integral sign), §3.6, and §4.7 can be shortened to highlights (e.g., Chebyshev, Cauchy–Schwarz, Jensen) if the class needs more time elsewhere.
- Sections 1.7–1.8, 2.5–2.6, 3.7–3.8, and 4.8–4.9 contain exercises and miscellanea for homework, discussion, and enrichment.
- Homework is assigned biweekly according to the syllabus; natural coverage endpoints are §§1.1–1.3 (Week 2), §§1.4–2.2 (Week 4), §§2.3–2.4 (Week 5, before the midterm), §§3.1–3.6 (Week 8), and §§4.1–4.7 (Week 10).
- The official UCR calendar lists November 11 and November 26–27 as holidays/closures. November 11 is a Wednesday and does not affect the regular lecture schedule; Thanksgiving cancels the Thursday, November 26 meeting.
